b Kintsugi program

Overview · Work done so far

Help us build b. b Kintsugi is our program for building b itself: bWalletX, bChatX and the b*X apps. The Bitcoin Corporation Ltd posts the work and pays developers directly, wallet to wallet. Each job is a microcontract with a fixed price, and you get the stated price in full. It works the other way too: users who want a fix or a feature can pay us to build it, and we build it or put it out as a Kintsugi job. Jobs between users belong on bJobsX, which takes a 20% fee. Verify your ID, show your setup, pass one entry task, and take paid work.

Kintsugi jobs also appear in bJobs. For other BSV-paid work, see bJobsX.com.

Who it is for

People who already work AI-first with Claude Code and want steady, small, well-defined jobs paid in Bitcoin: development, design, marketing, writing, testing. You choose which tasks to take and when to work.

How work arrives

From clients, through b

A user asks b for a fix or a feature. b writes it up as a task: what to change, how to test it, how long it should take and its fixed price. The client accepts the price and the job opens. Any Kintsugi fee is shown in b’s quote before you sign.

From bounties

People post bounties for features and fixes they want. Each one carries a tag your pull request quotes to claim it.

The pay

A fixed price for every job, paid in BSV

Each Kintsugi job is a microcontract with a fixed price, set before you start and written into the contract. Most Kintsugi work is paid for by The Bitcoin Corporation Ltd: on those jobs you get the stated price in full, straight to you. When someone else commissions a job through b, any Kintsugi fee is shown in b’s quote before you sign.

Converted at the BSV/USD rate on the day you are paid. When the work is accepted, the client pays you directly, from their wallet to your $handle in bWalletX, peer to peer. Kintsugi never holds the money.

Four steps

  1. A verified ID. Identity check by Veriff. We keep the result, not your documents.
  2. The right tools. A paid Anthropic Claude plan with Claude Code. No local models, no ChatGPT. Link your GitHub, declare your plan, and your Kintsugi commits carry the Co-Authored-By: Claude line.
  3. The entry test. One real task, done to spec in Claude Code and reviewed by us. Pass it and you are Kintsugi Level 1.
  4. Get certified. Levels go up with accepted work. Level 3 builders review others.

Two signatures on every job

The client

The Bitcoin Corporation Ltd. Sets the job and the price, and pays the developer directly when the work is accepted.

The developer

Builds it to the spec and opens the pull request.

Kintsugi

Checks the spec and reviews the work. Doesn’t sign, and can’t move or hold the money.

The client and the developer sign the same contract in bChatX before work starts. It sets the task, the price, the deadline and what counts as done. Delivered work belongs to the client once it is paid for, and is released under the Open BSV licence (code) and CC BY 4.0 (docs). If the client and developer disagree about whether the work is done, Kintsugi reviews it and decides.

Security tools you must run

Every pull request is checked before it is paid. You run these tools yourself, at your own cost. Tool and AI usage costs are yours; they are not added to the job’s price.

  • Shannon (KeygraphHQ, open source): AI penetration testing. Required for any change that touches sign-in, payments, keys or signing. You pay for the AI model usage it runs on. Run it only against your own local or test copy with test wallets, never against live sites or real funds.
  • Semgrep: static code analysis on every pull request.
  • gitleaks: secret scanning before every commit. A key, seed phrase or password in a commit means the work is rejected.
  • pnpm audit and OSV-Scanner: known holes in the packages you depend on.
  • CodeQL: deeper code analysis.
  • OWASP ZAP (baseline scan): for web pages and APIs.
  • Claude Code /security-review: on every pull request, using the Claude subscription you already need.

Attach the results to your pull request. Work without them is not reviewed or paid.

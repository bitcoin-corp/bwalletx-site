Airdrops. Tokens straight to your holders.

An airdrop is a token or NFT that arrives in your wallet without you buying or minting it. Issuers use airdrops to reward the people who hold their token. bWalletX is getting a proper place for them, and the safety rules that go with them.

Live since 5.1.85 Your airdrop address and the Airdrops inbox are in the app. Items still marked Coming are not built yet.

Your airdrop address

Every bWalletX account has an ordinals address: the address that holds your tokens and NFTs. That is your airdrop address. Give it to anyone who wants to send you a token.

  • Live since 5.1.85Your airdrop address, with a QR code, in Receive › Airdrops.
  • ComingThe same address and QR on your public bChatX page at bchatx.com/u/<name>, so issuers can find it.

The Airdrops inbox

Anything that turns up unasked goes to an inbox instead of mixing into your wallet. A token or NFT counts as an airdrop when it was paid to one of your addresses by a transaction you didn’t fund and didn’t start: anything you bought, minted, launched, recovered or swapped is not an airdrop.

  • Live since 5.1.85A badge counts new airdrops since you last opened the inbox.
  • Live since 5.1.85Keep moves an airdrop out of the inbox. Hide hides it, and every future airdrop from the same issuer.
  • Live since 5.1.85Only from issuers I kept before: a switch that shows airdrops only from issuers you have kept something from.
  • Live since 5.1.85Unverified labels. An airdrop shows who sent it (the token’s ID, or the sending address). We don’t check what a stranger’s token is or claims to be.

Stay safe

  • Never type your 12 words on a site an airdrop points to. A token’s name, picture or link can say anything. No real airdrop needs your recovery phrase. bWalletX never asks for it outside the wallet.
  • NFT content is sandboxed. HTML and SVG inscriptions are shown in an empty sandbox: no scripts, and no access to the wallet. SVG thumbnails are never loaded as wallet pages.
  • Address-poisoning warnings. Scammers send a tiny payment or an unsolicited token from an address that looks like one you pay (same first and last characters), hoping you copy it from History. When you send to an address like that, the wallet warns you and shows the address it resembles.

For issuers: airdrops, not promises

If you want to reward your holders, send them tokens. Don’t promise them returns, profits or a share of anything. A token that promises returns may be a security.

When you create a token in bWalletX, the wallet warns you while you type if the description promises returns, and won’t sign until that wording is gone: “Remove wording that promises returns before publishing. Describe airdrops or room access instead.” It is a word filter, not legal advice.

By default, holding a token gives you one thing: entry to that token’s bChat room. Anything else is what the issuer says, shown as “Issuer says” and not checked by bWalletX.

Airdrops that unlock over time are planned too: see Token locking.

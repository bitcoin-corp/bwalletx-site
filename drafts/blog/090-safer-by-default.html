Safer by default: what the reports taught us

A security audit and a steady stream of reports through b-Aware led to a long list of fixes. The short version: what you approve is exactly what happens, and nothing is granted that you didn’t see.

The wallet's own keys stay the wallet's

The most serious finding was that an outside site could ask for a key the wallet uses for itself, including the keys behind bTrust. Outside apps are now refused those keys before you’re even asked. Permission lines are no longer ticked for you.

What you see is what you grant

Approval sheets show every line they grant. If a request arrives while a sheet is open, it waits and gets its own sheet. A look-alike address such as tokenblaster.com.ab1.io is shown in full, never shortened to the name it imitates. If a site isn’t sure to be who it says, the sheet says “a site that claims to be”.

A plain sign-in no longer stores a monthly allowance. Instead, a site can only pay without asking when the payment fits that site’s envelope, and the money stays in your wallet until each payment is made. Forget the site and its envelope comes back to you.

Signing in and pairing

Sign in with bWalletX asks you to type the code the site shows, so a forwarded link can’t be approved blind. Pairing your phone uses only our own relay, each pairing keeps its own permissions, and new pairings compare an 8-digit code. Disconnecting a site removes its permissions.

Passwords and your words

Changing your password needs proof it’s you: your current password, Face ID or passcode, or your 12 words. New wallets use a much stronger password key, and older ones upgrade on the next unlock. On the web the unlock key stays in memory only. Recovery words are caught in reports and Ask b before they leave your device.

Paymail payments are checked against the person’s key before you sign, and pausing spending also stops standing orders and subscriptions.

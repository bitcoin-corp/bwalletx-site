Partnership shares.

Start something with friends. Share it fairly. Incorporate when you’re ready.

The path

Most things stay small, and that’s fine. Each step is optional. You only take the next one when you want to.

Token→Rooms→Partnership shares→Company→Private placements→One day: IPO

Token

Launch a token for your idea. People who like it can hold it.

Rooms

The token works as a ticket to your chat rooms. Your community gathers there.

Partnership shares

When a few of you start working on it together, record who owns what.

Company

Incorporate. Partnership shares become company shares, one for one.

Private placements

Raise money from people you choose, under the company’s own rules.

One day: IPO

A regulated public offering. Only then can shares trade in public.

An ice-cream story

How it will work. Locked partner shares and splits by set shares are not built yet. See Coming below.

Fred, Joe and you decide to sell ice cream, paid for in digital cash. You set up Partnership shares: Fred 30%, Joe 30%, you 40%.

Fred 27
Joe 27
You 36
Max 10

After Max joins: everyone gives up a tenth, so 30/30/40 becomes 27/27/36, plus Max 10.

  • Locked together. The shares will be locked, so nobody can sell theirs without the others agreeing.
  • Max joins. Max has freezers. The three of you agree to admit him for 10%.
  • Every sale splits itself. Money from each sale will go straight to each partner by their share. No spreadsheet.
  • Incorporate later. When the business grows, you form a company. Each partnership share becomes one company share.

b drafts it with you

The partners get a shared chat that only they are in. b, the bWalletX assistant, is in it too.

b drafts your partnership agreement, and any change to it. b explains each term in plain words. Anything still open is marked [TO BE AGREED], so you can settle it point by point. Each of you accepts with your own wallet. Help with disputes after signing is coming; see Contracts in chat.

When you all agree, each partner signs with their own wallet. Nobody signs for anyone else.

b is not a lawyer. Drafts are for the partners to read and agree. For bigger stakes, take advice from a solicitor or accountant.

Know what a partnership is

Until you incorporate, you are a partnership. Profits are shared. Each partner is personally liable for the business’s debts. Each partner pays tax on their own share.

Partnership shares are never offered to the public. Public trading comes only after a regulated offering.

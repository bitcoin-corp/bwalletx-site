Identity. Verified when you need it. Private when you don’t.

Your wallet doesn’t need your name. Some things do. When one of them comes up, you verify once, and your wallet can then show that a real person stands behind it without carrying your details around.

Looking for names, $handles and sign-in? They’re on Handles.

Why some things need a real identity

Most of the wallet works with no name at all. A few things are different, because the other side has to know who they are dealing with:

  • Signing a contract that has to bind a real person.
  • Investing in a private placement.
  • Services that are regulated and must know their customer.

You are only asked when something needs it.

Verify once

The check is run by Veriff, through bit-sign. You show an ID document and take a selfie in Veriff’s flow. When Veriff approves, bit-sign records the result and your wallet can fetch a certificate that says you are verified.

  • LiveID check with Veriff, through bit-sign.
  • LiveCertificate in your wallet. Your wallet proves it owns its key, and bit-sign issues a certificate to that key.

Who sees what

  • VeriffYour document and selfie. Veriff handles them in its own flow.
  • bit-signThe result. bit-sign keeps your name, date of birth and document type, and checks your name against sanctions lists.
  • Your walletA certificate with no name and no date of birth. It says you are verified, whether you are over 18, the country of your document, the check level and its dates. Each of those is encrypted, so the wallet can show one without the others.
  • On chainNothing personal. Your name, date of birth and documents are not put on chain.

Who can see the result, and when

  • In a bit-sign deal room, a host can ask a named member for a check. The member chooses to accept. The room then sees that the check was submitted, and whether it was approved or declined. It does not see your name, date of birth or documents.
  • bit-sign’s signed record of your check is private. Only you, signed in, can see all of it: the check level, the type and country of your document, the sanctions result and the date. It has no name and no date of birth. Anyone else who looks it up with your key gets “not found”, the same answer as for a key with no check, so they can’t even tell you’ve been checked.
  • Sharing part of it with one person is your choice: you sign a grant naming the fields, the person’s key and an end date, and they see only those fields until then. The same goes for an issuer asking bit-sign whether you meet its rules: without your grant it gets “not found”, and with one it sees only the checks your grant covers. The sanctions result is shown to them only as pass or fail, and never to the public.

Using it

  • LiveSign and seal. Sign .NDS.HTML contracts on bit-sign with your drawn signature and your wallet key in one step, sealed on chain, from the Chrome extension or a paired phone. Only hashes go on chain, never the document. If a paid seal is interrupted, it can be recovered rather than paid twice. More: /sign.
  • LiveChecks in deal rooms. A counterparty sees approved or declined, not your documents.
  • ComingShowing one fact from your wallet. The certificate is built so the wallet can reveal a single field, such as over 18, to an app. A finished flow for that is still being built.
  • ComingThe rest of the investor journey. One-tap pay from the wallet and a single investor flow are still being built.

Deleting your data

  • LiveDelete account in the wallet’s Settings removes your bit-sign account, handle, linked accounts and chats. The records of an identity check are kept, because anti-money-laundering rules require identity checks to be kept for a period.
  • ComingSelf-serve deletion of your check. Not built yet. Until it is, ask support@bwalletx.com.
  • NoteAnything on chain cannot be deleted by anyone. That is why nothing personal is put there.

Keys

Your keys are made and kept on your device. They are not sent to us. Lose your 12 words and nobody, including us, can recover the account.
